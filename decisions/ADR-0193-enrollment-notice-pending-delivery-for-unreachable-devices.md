## Context

Approval today (`approve_inner`) signs a notice and requires `deliver_enrolled` to queue it into a session with every other non-quarantined device; if any one has no deliverable path the approval is refused with nothing changed. As read, "deliverable" means Core can encrypt to that device through some active conversation (it holds the device's key record and a route), not that the device is online: a device that is merely offline is served by the relay queue. What blocks a link is a device with no such path: a sibling that has never completed a session through any conversation (for example a second device on an account with no conversations yet), one whose key record is missing or whose routes are all dead. The person cannot link a third device until that sibling is reached or removed. Two further gaps in the same code: queued delivery is never acknowledged, and the relay holds an item only for its own retention, so a sibling offline longer than that never learns of the enrollment without anyone noticing; and a sibling that was quarantined at approval gets no notice at all, then cannot advance when restored because `stage_enrollment` demands a verified notice for every unseen enrollment.

The audited text fixes the property (every other active device gets an authenticated, non-suppressible notice; a link is never silent) and is silent on timing; an offline device cannot receive anything at the moment of approval under any design, and the existing code already counts "queued at the relay" as delivery. This ADR makes the eventual-delivery model explicit, durable and acknowledged, instead of refusing.

## Decision

1. **Approval no longer requires a path now.** For each other device the approving head lists as active (control or messaging, not quarantined) Core records a durable `OwedNotice { sibling_device_id, enrollment_state_hash, notice_digest, created_at, attempts, last_attempt_at, state }` in the same transaction that commits the `link_messaging` successor and the local notice record. The transaction fails as a whole (nothing changes, as today) if the owed records cannot be stored, if more than 32 notices would be owed at once (section 6), or if the notice cannot be built.
2. **States.** `Queued` (a session send was accepted by the relay), `Waiting` (no path yet), `Acknowledged` (the sibling answered, section 3), `Held` (the sibling is quarantined; delivery is owed from the moment it is active again), `Dropped` (the sibling is revoked or removed by a verified head; the record is deleted). A Queued item that stays unacknowledged is re-sent, not trusted.
3. **Acknowledgement.** A sibling that accepts a `KIND_ENROLLED` payload (it staged the chain and notices, committed them and recorded the person-visible notice) replies, through the same session, with a new control payload `KIND_ENROLLED_ACK` (13): `{0: 13, 1: notice_digest}` for every notice it newly applied or already held. The digest is SHA-256 of the signed notice bytes. The sender marks `Acknowledged` only for a digest it owes to that device; any other digest is ignored. The ack carries no authority and changes no state but the sender's owed list.
4. **Retry.** The approver tries again: on any inbound payload from that sibling; when a path to it appears (`ensure_dev` starts to succeed, a route or key record arrives); at app start; and on a local schedule of 15 minutes, 1 hour, 6 hours, then every 24 hours. The payload is rebuilt at send time from the stored chain and notices. A sibling more than `MAX_CHAIN_TAIL` states behind receives several payloads in chain order, each carrying at most `MAX_CHAIN_TAIL` states and the notice of every enrollment those states add, so contiguous coverage is complete. There is no give-up: an owed notice is never dropped for age (sections 4 and 5 are how it ends).
5. **Any holder may carry it.** A notice is self-authenticating (signed by a control device of the named state), so a device that holds it, including the newly linked one once it can reach a sibling, SHOULD forward the same `KIND_ENROLLED` payload and treat an ack the same way. This keeps delivery alive if the approving device is lost.
6. **Bounds.** The owed list is bounded by the device limits (at most 63 other devices) and by 32 notices; a notice owed to an unacknowledged device is never evicted by the existing `MAX_NOTICES` trim of the display list (that list and the owed list are separate stores). At the bound a new approval is refused with `Quota` and the message "Some of your devices haven't been told about earlier changes yet. Reconnect or remove one first" (section 8).
7. **What a sibling does without a notice (fails closed).** Unchanged and now stated: a sibling that has not applied a verified notice for an enrollment does not advance its head across it (`stage_enrollment` returns none), does not treat the new device as authorized for itself, and rejects traffic from a device its head does not list (K5). It never learns of the enrollment silently from anywhere but a verified notice. When a verified notice arrives, however late, it applies, the person-visible notice appears on that sibling at that time, and it acknowledges.
8. **Strict option (opt-in, strongest).** A local setting on a control device, "Only add a device when all my other devices can be told right away" (default off), restores today's behavior for approvals made on that device: approval is refused (nothing changed) unless every other active device has a deliverable path at that moment. The setting is local policy of the approving device and travels in no account-private record; it never weakens anything the default does.
9. **Quarantined siblings.** A sibling quarantined at approval gets an `OwedNotice` in `Held`. When a verified head shows it active again, Core moves it to `Waiting` and delivers it, so a restored device can catch up.
10. **Visibility on the approving device.** The Devices list marks every device with an owed, unacknowledged notice ("Not told yet"), with the time of the last attempt, an action to try now, and the existing action to remove that device. The new device appears in the list immediately, as today, with the approver's notice. The state is read from Core (`DevicesView`), never composed by the UI.
11. **No silent link anywhere.** A link is complete on the approving device when it has committed the successor, the signed notice and the owed list. It is not "announced" to a sibling until that sibling acknowledges; every device that has not is listed as such on the approver until it does or is removed. No client may hide, clear or auto-resolve an owed item.

## Where did authority go?

Nowhere new. The authority to add the device is unchanged (one parent-authorized `link_messaging` successor); the notice is the existing signed, frozen W0 object; each sibling still verifies the notice and the chain itself before it advances or shows anything, and still refuses everything else. The queue and the ack carry no authority. The Home sees only sealed session traffic (as it does for `KIND_ENROLLED` today). A compromised or lost approver can delay notices; any holder can carry them (decision 5) and the person can remove a device from any other control device.

## Consequences

Linking works whenever the approving device can sign, with the other devices told as soon as they can be (typically at once through the relay queue, as today, and later otherwise). The window in which a sibling does not yet know is explicit and visible on the approver instead of being a refusal; a person worried about that window turns on the strict option. Residual: a rogue approval on an unlocked phone is visible to the owner on other devices only once those devices are reachable, as with the relay queue today; the control remains the person's attention to "Not told yet" and the Devices list, and Hardened's second confirmation (ENDPOINT_COERCION section 2, item 3) is unchanged. Versions that predate `KIND_ENROLLED_ACK` ignore it and never acknowledge; an approver treats that as "no answer yet" and shows it, and never clears it silently. If the operator reads "finalizes without that notice" in ACCOUNT_CONTINUITY section 12 as requiring the notice to have reached every device before the approver commits, this ADR departs from that reading for the default and keeps it as the strict option; that choice is the one to confirm.

## Work list for a builder

1. **State** (`state.rs`): `OwedNotice` and its state enum; persisted in the profile state with `#[serde(default)]`; migration: none needed (empty). Strict flag `link_strict_notice`, default false, device-local.
2. **Code** (`flows/ceremony.rs`): `approve_inner` builds the owed list instead of returning `Unavailable` for a missing path (strict flag keeps `Unavailable`); a single transaction with the successor, notice record and owed records (the existing snapshot/restore covers it); `on_enrolled` sends `KIND_ENROLLED_ACK` after commit; new handler for `KIND_ENROLLED_ACK` (sibling only, digest must be owed to that sender); retry driver on the triggers of decision 4; `Held` to `Waiting` on a verified restore; `Dropped` on a verified revoke; multi-payload chain splitting; forwarding by any holder. Add the ack kind number to the control-kind table (13 was free in the code at the time of this ADR; check `KIND_*` before use).
3. **Types and views** (`api.rs`, `packages/om-platform-api`, harness): `DeviceSummary.noticeState` = `told | owed | held`, `lastAttemptAt?`; intents `RetryDeviceNotice { deviceId }` (no other new intent; removal reuses the existing one); strict option as a setting intent.
4. **Tests:** link with one sibling that has no path: approval commits, owed `Waiting`, later path appears, delivered, acked, record cleared; link with an offline routable sibling: queued, re-sent after a simulated relay loss, acked; sibling never acked: stays visible, never cleared, retry schedule observed; two siblings, one acks one not; approver restarts mid-wait; approver lost and the new device forwards; sibling 6 enrollments behind (chain splitting) catches up in order; sibling quarantined at approval is `Held`, restored, catches up; revoke drops the record; bound of 32 refused with `Quota`; strict option refuses with nothing changed; a sibling given the new device's traffic before the notice rejects it; an ack for a digest not owed is ignored; an unknown control kind 13 on an older client is ignored. The 16,200-case ACS differential and `device_link_no_notice.rs` stay green.
5. **Vectors:** none required for the ack (it carries a digest of an existing object); add the ack and the multi-payload case to the Core integration vectors. No wire registry change; if a control-kind table is added to `spec/`, register 13 there (design-bound path: read the printed domains, carry the trailers).
6. **Model:** extend V-021's failing trace: "a link completes while some active device is neither acknowledged nor listed as owed on the approver" must be rejected. Do not mark any gate passed on the author's own model run.
7. **UI copy keys** (`apps/web/src/copy/en.ts`, devices section): `devicesCopy.notTold` ("Not told yet"), `devicesCopy.notToldDetail(label)` ("{label} hasn't heard about the new device yet. OM will tell it when it connects."), `devicesCopy.tryNow` ("Try now"), `devicesCopy.removeDevice` (existing), `devicesCopy.heldNotice` ("{label} is paused. It will be told when you turn it back on."), `devicesCopy.noticeLate` (on the sibling, "A device was added to your account earlier: {label}, {time}. If this was not you, open it and remove it."), `devicesCopy.strictOption` and its one-line help, `devicesCopy.tooManyOwed` (decision 6).

## UX line

Adding a device just works; any device that is off or out of reach shows "Not told yet" until it hears about it, and you can remove it from the list at any time.
