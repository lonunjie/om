# OM — Name, Meanings and Product Promise

- **Status:** Canonical project identity
- **Checkpoint:** 2026-09-23 07:56 PHT

OM stays **OM** because the short name intentionally carries several compatible meanings. These meanings are product constraints, not marketing trivia.

The letter **O** may also be read as **0**: **0M**. This is intentional secondary symbolism, not a rename.

## Open-source Messenger

OM is a gift to humanity in the tradition of durable open commons such as Git and Linux. The protocol, normative artifacts and reference implementation must remain independently usable, forkable and improvable under the project's open licensing architecture.

## OpenMessenger

OM is not a walled garden. Independent clients can implement the public protocol without permission. Interoperation with services, bots, LLM agents, bridges and other clients is an architectural goal, subject to explicit security boundaries and user consent.

## OwnMessenger

Users should be able to make OM their own: identity, infrastructure, client, themes, expressive media, automations and optional extensions can be replaced or customized without weakening the security floor or requiring a central OM account.

## OurMessenger

OM must work well for people who gather around something together: friends, families, teams, organizations, communities and businesses. Shared spaces may carry their own roles, rules, style, channels/rooms, services and governance without turning the OM Project into their operator.

## OneMessenger

OM should be capable of growing into the messenger a user does not need to replace merely because a different group wants a different feature. Optional features belong behind interoperable extensions/plugins and capability negotiation rather than forcing every installation to carry every feature or every community to depend on one vendor.

“One” is an aspiration toward breadth and interoperability, not a claim that competing messengers must disappear or that OM may centralize control to win adoption.

## OhMessenger

OM should feel approachable, lively and human. The underlying architecture may be sophisticated; ordinary people should experience a familiar, pleasant messenger rather than infrastructure software.

## 0M — zero total control

The **O** in OM can also be read as **0**: **0M**.

Zero does **not** mean zero servers, zero governance, zero moderators, zero businesses or zero trust assumptions. Real systems need operators and authorities in bounded places.

It means:

> **zero parties have total control of OM.**

No founder, SeedLife, company, government, cloud, app store, directory, bot catalog, provider, dominant client, repository host or protocol extension should become the one party whose permission is required for OM to continue as a usable interoperable commons.

0M therefore expresses **decentralization of ultimate control**: OM is for and by everyone who implements, operates, contributes to and uses it. Authorities may exist, but they remain scoped, replaceable and unable to compose into universal ownership of the network.

The target is not theatrical decentralization or a mandatory blockchain. It is practical anti-capture:

- identity can outlive providers;
- services/bots can be created without a central issuer;
- directories/catalogs can compete;
- clients can be independent;
- communities govern themselves;
- infrastructure can move;
- protocol artifacts can be mirrored/forked;
- no commercial success turns into protocol privilege.

## Interpretation

When a product decision is ambiguous, the seven meanings together imply:

1. keep the commons open;
2. interoperate instead of enclosing;
3. let users safely customize;
4. support shared social/organizational spaces;
5. prefer extensible capability over product fragmentation;
6. preserve a simple, delightful default experience; and
7. keep total control at zero: no indispensable universal controller.

The public name remains **OM**. `0M` is a philosophical reading/symbol, not a required product spelling. Expanded meanings may be explained contextually; no single expansion is required as the permanent consumer-facing subtitle.
